    About Paperscope

    Paperscope searches free scholarly databases, merges the results and ranks them against your query and, if you add one, your research profile. It is not Google Scholar and does not search the same sources or reproduce its ranking. It is free to use and needs no paid AI service.

    Cost and limits

    Paperscope has no server: your browser asks the databases directly, so everyone gets their own free allowance and one person’s searching never uses up anyone else’s. OpenAlex meters its free API in dollars: without a key you get about $0.10 of free use a day, with a free key $1 a day. A search here usually uses $0.002–0.003, so that is roughly 30–50 searches a day without a key, or 300–500 with one; looking up an author uses about the same, and browsing citations or loading more results uses a little more. Paperscope shows what each search used. Without a key the allowance appears to be per network address, so people on the same campus or office network may share it; a free key avoids that. When the allowance runs out, OpenAlex refuses requests until midnight UTC; Paperscope keeps searching Crossref and Europe PMC and marks the results as incomplete. Nobody is billed unless they have bought prepaid credit on openalex.org. Crossref, Europe PMC and ORCID are free and need no key, but may slow down very busy users.

    Ranking method

    Compare them on your own graded searches under Profile › Graded searches.

    AI answers (optional)

    Paperscope can ask an AI model to write a short answer to your search from the abstracts of the top papers, citing each one. It uses your own account with the provider, which bills you directly; Paperscope adds no charge. Your key is kept in this browser only.

    Any service with an OpenAI-style /chat/completions endpoint. A model running on your own computer costs nothing, but it must allow requests from this page.
